## Read-out

- **Dropping the ATR stop and keeping 100 EMA + a fixed 20% stop** is the loosest exit — positions are only cut on a weekly close below the 100-week EMA (the 20% floor rarely binds once price is up). Highest exposure and usually the highest CAGR, but the deepest drawdowns, so Calmar tends to *fall* vs the full ATR system.
- **100 EMA + a 20% *trailing* stop** re-introduces a profit-locking trail (a coarse substitute for the ATR line). It cuts drawdown vs the EMA-only version and generally restores Calmar, at some cost to CAGR.
- **A stop alone** (either 20% variant, no EMA) exits latest of all and rides the biggest drawdowns — highest raw return in a bull sample, worst risk-adjusted.

**Takeaway:** the ATR (or a 20% trailing) line is what controls drawdown; remove every trailing component and you keep more upside but a rougher equity curve and a lower Calmar. If you want to drop ATR, pair the 100 EMA with a 20% *trailing* stop rather than only the fixed one.
