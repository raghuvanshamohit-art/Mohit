# From Easy Money to a System: Rakesh Pujara's Trading Journey

*A conversation between **Vivek Bajaj** (co-founder, StockEdge & Elearnmarkets) and **Rakesh Pujara** on why rule-based, system-driven trading — not gut feel — is what actually compounds capital.*

---

## Why this conversation

Over the last several videos I (Vivek) have been repeating one idea again and
again: **change your trading style into a rule-based style.** It's good to have
market understanding, but understanding alone doesn't pay — making money
consistently is only possible through rules and a system you can follow.

I know it takes more than one video for this to sink in, so I've been looking
for guests who have actually lived this transition — people who started
somewhere else and *evolved* into system-based traders, and who now help others
do the same.

Rakesh Pujara is exactly that person. His path runs from **arbitrage →
discretionary trading → system-based trading**, and today he also influences
others to adopt the same discipline. A journey very similar to my own.

## The journey

### 1991–1992 — The market pulls me in
My journey started in **1991**, when I was a B.Com student. If you remember,
**1992 was the Harshad Mehta era** — that frenzy is what pulled me toward the
market. I never had much money, so I borrowed from friends and speculated.

When the Harshad Mehta scam broke, I lost **₹2–3 lakhs**. That was my **first
real lesson**: never speculate blindly, without thinking. Earning that lost
money back took a long time, and it taught me a rule I still hold — **one
mistake can set you back five steps.**

### The turn toward professional learning
That loss changed my approach. I decided that anything done with *professional
learning* behind it is always better than acting on impulse. So I studied
properly — took formal courses, including financial management — instead of
treating the market as a casino.

### 1999 — Naman Securities and the arbitrage years
In **1999** I got my break at **Naman Securities** and was made **finance
head**. That's when I realised there was genuinely good, repeatable money to be
made here — and I started with **arbitrage**.

Back then there was **no uniform settlement**: NSE settled on one day, BSE on
another (Tuesday vs. Friday). That mismatch created a near-risk-free spread —
you could capture roughly **2% a week** on the exchange difference. Compounded,
that's an extraordinary return with very little risk. It felt like a dream come
true: money on the table, week after week.

### 2001 — The Ketan Parekh crash and losing the easy edge
Then came the **2001 crash (the Ketan Parekh episode)**. People lost faith in
the market. Around **2001–2002**, **uniform settlement** was introduced across
exchanges — and just like that, the arbitrage edge I had relied on simply
**vanished**. The easy money was gone, and I now needed to rebuild capital to
become a **discretionary trader**.

### Building a proprietary desk
I brought many traders onto the **proprietary desk** at Naman — a lot of good
people were working the prop desk. BSE, in particular, was highly
unpredictable back then. Between **2004 and 2007** a lot of traders made
money, and they started to believe that this run would simply continue.

### 2008 — The lesson that made me a systems trader
Then **2008** happened. It's a crash we lived through, and it hurt. The hard
truth: **about 90% of my "good" traders turned into the bottom 10%.** People
who thought skill alone would carry them through discovered how quickly an
unmanaged book can unravel.

That was the turning point. Discretion — however talented — wasn't enough.
**Rules were.**

## What system-based trading actually is

It is **not** a claim that only system traders make money and everyone else
loses. It's simpler than that: in system-based trading, **the rules are framed
in advance in your mind, and you follow them** — every time, regardless of how
you feel that day.

The core ideas:

- **Rules first.** Define your entries, exits, and position sizing *before* you
  trade, and then obey them.
- **Risk sized to income.** Risk is calculated in proportion to your annual
  income — you never bet more than your situation can absorb.
- **A good strike rate,** achieved by trading only the setups your system
  actually validates.
- **A defined cycle.** Much of this can be run on a weekly rhythm — e.g. a
  **Friday-to-Friday** structure — which is exactly where undisciplined
  traders struggle, because it removes the temptation to react intraday.
- **Instruments that suit it.** It works well on liquid, well-behaved
  instruments like **NIFTY and Bank NIFTY.**
- **Multiple systems.** Once you're mature, you don't run one system — you run
  **several** in parallel, each with its own edge.

If you follow rules and trade sensibly, the market becomes far *easier* to
handle. And this is where **algo trading** helps: automation is simply the
machinery that lets you execute a system faithfully, which makes rule-based
trading accessible to everyone.

---

## The takeaway

Nobody hands you a system. **Everyone has to do their own hard work** — study
properly, write down your rules, size your risk to what your life can absorb,
and then follow the system through good weeks and bad.

The market kept teaching Rakesh the same lesson at every stage — 1992, 2001,
2008 — until the lesson finally became a method:

> **Understanding earns you respect. Rules earn you money.**
